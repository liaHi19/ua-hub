# UA Hub — Project Plan

\*Bristol-Odesa Association

## What we're building

A simple website where anyone in the UK can find Ukrainian community events — concerts, support groups, language classes, fundraisers, kids' activities — in their city or any other UK city, all in one place.

Organizers post their events once. The community sees everything together.

## The problem we're solving

The Ukrainian community in the UK lives on Telegram and WhatsApp groups. Each city has its own. Each charity runs its own. Each language class has its own.

**The result:**

- A Ukrainian living in Bristol sees Bristol events. They do not see what is happening in Manchester, London, or Edinburgh — even when they would happily travel for a meaningful event.
- Events get lost in the scroll. Someone checking a busy WhatsApp group on Sunday night cannot find what is happening next weekend, let alone next month.
- Organizers announce the same event in five groups, hoping the right people see it. Some do. Many don't.
- Newcomers to a city have no way in. They don't know which Telegram groups exist or how to join them.

Nobody has built the obvious thing: **one place, for everyone, for the whole country.**

## Why we're the ones to do it

The Bristol-Odesa Association has the network, the legitimacy, and the motivation. We do not need permission or funding to start — the first version costs nothing to run.

## How it works, in plain language

**The journey shifts over time.** At the start, Telegram brings people in — they click an event link shared in a group and discover the site. Over time, as the platform fills with events, services, and organizations, people come to the site _directly_ — because it is the fastest place to find what they need. Telegram becomes one entry point among many, not the only one.

For a community member — early days:

1. They see an event link shared in a Telegram group — for example, a concert in Manchester.
2. They click. The page shows: when, where, who is organising, a poster, a short description in English and Ukrainian.
3. They notice the site has more — other cities, other dates, online events. They browse a little.
4. They share useful events back into their own Telegram groups, completing the loop.

For a community member — later, once the platform is established:

1. They open the site directly because they need something specific — "what is happening in Edinburgh this weekend," "is there a Ukrainian-speaking accountant in Leeds," "which charities run Saturday schools in London."
2. They find it in seconds. No scrolling through Telegram archives, no asking around in five groups, no hoping someone replies.
3. The site becomes a tool they reach for, the way people reach for Google Maps when they need directions. Quiet, reliable, always there.

The shift from "Telegram brings them in" to "they come directly" is the most important measure of long-term success. It means the community has internalised the site as part of how it operates.

For an organizer:

1. They submit an event through a short form.
2. We review it within three days and publish it.
3. They get a permanent link they can share into Telegram. The link unfolds into a clean, designed preview card — much better than a hand-typed Telegram post.
4. The event appears alongside every other UK Ukrainian event, where new audiences can find it.

In future, trusted users and verified organizations will be able to publish directly — no waiting for review. Once a person or an org has earned trust over time (a track record of accurate, appropriate submissions), the friction is removed. They post, the event appears immediately, the community sees it within seconds. Moderation is the trust mechanism for newcomers; established voices skip the queue.

For the Bristol-Odesa Association:

1. We maintain the platform. We are the moderators.
2. We onboard new partner organizations one at a time.
3. We carry the credibility of being "the place" for UK Ukrainian community events.

## Why this is the right time

Three reasons:

1. **The demand is proven.** Telegram groups already exist and are active. We are not creating demand from nothing — we are giving an existing audience a better tool.
2. **The cost to try is essentially zero.** Modern web tools let one volunteer build, seed, and launch this in twelve weeks of evenings. Hosting is free at our scale.
3. **No one else is doing it.** Eventbrite is generic. Facebook events are dead in this community. Telegram groups will keep fragmenting until someone steps up. That someone is us.

## Timeline

Twelve weeks total, working evenings. We are deliberately taking a realistic pace — solo evening projects with optimistic timelines fail. Three months gives room for the build, the inevitable mid-project setbacks, and a proper seeding phase before any public link goes out.

| Weeks | Goal                                                                                                                     | What stakeholders see                              |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| 1–3   | Foundation — set up the platform, sign-in, the submit form, the admin moderation queue                                   | Nothing public yet                                 |
| 4–6   | The public surface — event pages, the filterable list, the designed share card, cross-device testing                     | Nothing public yet                                 |
| 7–8   | Buffer — the part of every project where unexpected work appears. Built in deliberately, not optimistically.             | Nothing public yet                                 |
| 9–10  | Seed events — enter 20+ events from 3+ partner organizations across 3+ UK cities                                         | Soft launch — the site goes live with real content |
| 11    | Quiet launch — share into a small number of trusted Telegram groups, watch what happens, fix anything that breaks        | Soft public launch                                 |
| 12    | Wider launch — once the soft launch holds up, share into the broader Bristol / UK Ukrainian Telegram and WhatsApp groups | Public launch                                      |

Month 1 after launch: we measure whether the community is using it.
Month 3 after launch: we decide what to build next based on what we have learned.

**Why three months and not one:**

- Building, testing, and seeding properly takes time.
- A soft launch followed by a wider launch catches problems on a small audience first. If the site is broken, twenty people in a private group see it, not two thousand strangers.
- A deliberate buffer (Weeks 7–8) is built in. Without it, the first surprise blows the timeline. With it, surprises are absorbed and the launch date holds.
- Partner organizations need time to give us permission to seed their events, get back to us with logo files, agree on listing details. None of that runs at "one evening" speed.

## What success looks like

We are setting realistic targets, not vanity numbers.

**Launch day (end of Week 12):**

- At least 20 real events live, from at least 3 partner organizations, across at least 3 UK cities.
- A Telegram share of any event link produces a clean, designed preview card.
- The site works on phones, tablets, and computers.

**One month after launch:**

- At least 5 events submitted by people we did not personally invite.
- At least one partner organization contacts us to thank us, request a change, or submit more events.
- At least one event link gets forwarded between Telegram groups in different cities — proof that the cross-city value works.

**Three months after launch:**

- 50+ approved events from 5+ UK cities.
- We have a clear answer to: "Is this a useful thing, or did we build it for nobody?"

If month 3 after launch looks weak, we have learned cheaply. The cost to try was three months of one volunteer's evenings and £5 for a domain name. If it looks strong, we have a foundation to invite partner charities to become co-owners.

## What we are deliberately not doing in version 1

Saying "no" early protects the project from collapsing under its own ambition.

- **No paid features.** This is a community service, not a business. Paid tiers can come later if there is a reason.
- **No app to download.** A website that works perfectly on a phone is faster to build and serves more people.
- **No social features** (comments, likes, attendee lists). Telegram already does this; we are not competing.
- **No service directory or organization directory yet.** Events first. Other content types come once events are working. (See "The bigger picture" below for what this becomes.)
- **No automatic translation.** Organizers provide content in English and optionally Ukrainian. Machine translation creates more problems than it solves for a community that cares about language.

## The bigger picture — where this grows

Events are the wedge, not the destination. Once the platform has earned the community's trust as "the place where UK Ukrainian events live," two adjacent content types unlock naturally.

### Services — Ukrainians promoting Ukrainian services

The UK Ukrainian community is now permanent enough that there is real economic activity inside it. Ukrainian-run businesses, professionals, and skilled workers exist in every major city — but they are invisible to the community that would gladly use them.

A community member looking for a Ukrainian-speaking dentist in Manchester, a Ukrainian translator for an NHS appointment, a hairdresser who understands the right haircut for a child raised speaking Ukrainian, a tutor for their kids, a tax adviser who has seen Ukrainian refugee cases before — today, all of this happens through fragmented word-of-mouth in Telegram groups. Same problem as events: lost in scroll, fragmented by city.

UA Hub becomes the directory:

- **For service providers:** a clean listing page with their service, area, languages spoken, contact details, and a shareable link they can put on a business card or in their Telegram bio. Verified by the platform as a real Ukrainian-run business.
- **For community members:** a searchable list by city, service type, and language — "Ukrainian-speaking dentists in Greater Manchester" should be one click away, not a forum post.
- **For Bristol-Odesa:** the same trust mechanism that powers event moderation extends here. We are not certifying quality — we are confirming that the listing is genuinely from a Ukrainian or Ukrainian-friendly business and is not a scam.

This is a _huge_ outcome for the community. It turns the platform from "where I find events" into "where the UK Ukrainian community sees itself."

### Organizations — every UK Ukrainian charity in one place

There are dozens of UK charities and community associations supporting the Ukrainian community: Association of Ukrainians in Great Britain, Ukrainian Welcome Centre, Bristol-Odesa, Stand With Ukraine groups, language schools, Saturday schools, cultural societies. Each runs its own Telegram, its own Facebook page, its own newsletter.

A newcomer to the UK has no map of who exists or how to find them. A long-time community member knows the three or four orgs they have personal contact with — and is invisible to the rest.

UA Hub becomes the directory of organizations themselves:

- **For each organization:** a profile page — who they are, where they operate, what they do, how to contact them, and a live feed of their upcoming events pulled directly from the events system they are already using on the platform.
- **For community members:** a clear answer to "what organizations exist in my city?" and "who is doing work I want to support or get involved with?"
- **For partner charities:** mutual visibility. An org doing language classes in Edinburgh becomes visible to a family that just moved there. The community grows tighter.

Crucially, this works because of the events foundation. An org with a live page that shows three upcoming events feels alive. An org page with no events feels dead. The events directory feeds the org directory; they are designed to support each other.

## Risks we are watching, and how we handle them

| Risk                                            | Likelihood      | What we do about it                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Empty directory on launch                       | Medium          | Seed 20+ events from known partners before any public link goes out. A directory with 3 events looks dead.                                                                   |
| Organizers don't bother submitting              | Medium          | We do the first 20 ourselves, manually, with each partner's permission. Submission becomes self-service in month 2 if there is demand.                                       |
| Spam or abusive submissions                     | Low             | Every event is reviewed by a human admin before it is published. Five-submission-per-day limit per user.                                                                     |
| The technology breaks at scale                  | Low at our size | The free tier of the hosting service handles thousands of events. We have years before we outgrow it.                                                                        |
| Only Bristol uses it                            | Real risk       | This is why "events from at least 3 cities at launch" is non-negotiable. We will not publicly launch if we have only Bristol content.                                        |
| One volunteer running it gets burnt out or busy | Real risk       | The platform is designed to keep running with zero maintenance. Approving events takes minutes per week. Long term, we invite a small group of moderators from partner orgs. |

## Open questions for stakeholders

Things we need a decision on, but do not need to solve today.

1. **Domain name.** Options under consideration: `ua-hub.org.uk`, `uahub.uk`, `bristol-ua.org.uk`. We lean toward the first because it is national in tone, not Bristol-specific.
2. **Who is the public contact?** The email shown on the site for "contact us" needs to go to someone reliable.
3. **Policy on event content.** What do we publish? What do we decline? Specifically: political events, religious events, fundraisers for specific military causes. We need a one-page policy before launch so moderation decisions are consistent.
4. **Which partner organizations do we approach first** to seed events? We are aiming for 3 cities at launch.

## Why this matters

The UK Ukrainian community is bigger and more permanent than it was in 2022. People have settled. Children are starting school. Adults are building careers. Communities are forming — but they are forming in silos, fragmented by platform and by city.

A simple shared calendar is the smallest possible step toward a community that knows itself across the whole country. It is not glamorous. It is not innovative. It is the obvious thing nobody has built. We can build it in three-four months of evenings for £5 a year, and we are best placed to do it.

That is the case. The rest is execution.
